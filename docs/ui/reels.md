# The design language from the saved reels

Richard's saved "Design" reels, watched on 2026-11-22, summarised for the
horizontal city. This is the tracked copy of the working notes, without
accounts or links. The spec
(`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-22-horizontal-city-design.md`, "Sources")
says how each idea was taken in.

## What each reel teaches

1. **UX laws.** Closure: show a slice of the next card, so people know to
   go on. Figure-ground: dim what sits behind a dialog. Connectedness:
   lines that join steps show the order. Choice overload: offer a few
   choices, not many.
2. **"Unslop" checklist (20 points).** Drop purple gradients and gradient
   backgrounds. Use consistent icons, no emoji. Don't use Inter or an
   italic serif word. No headline badge, no two-button hero, no stat
   banner, no 1-2-3 steps. Match outer and inner radii. Fix contrast. Cut
   glowing shadows and coloured card borders. Break up identical cards.
   Glass only with a reason. Go easy on all-caps. Fix text wrapping.
   Restyle library defaults. Don't fade in every section. Use your own
   favicon and meta tags.
3. **Backgrounds that aren't flat.** Paintings, single-hue landscapes,
   blueprint or dot grids, grain. Use one or two, not many.
4. **A tactile password screen.** Reward early, punish late. Validate
   inline. Chunk long strings. Materiality: a texture should have a
   reason.
5. **19 principles of UX and animation.** These are the same as
   `docs/ui/ux-motion-principles.md`: staggering, slow in and slow out,
   interruptible motion, rubber banding, responding within 400 ms, UI
   motion under 300 ms, origin awareness, follow-through, frequency of use
   and peak-end.
6. **A stripped-down code screen.** Cut decoration first. Shorten the
   copy. Put one decorative shape at the edge, not behind the content.
   Make a distinct success moment.
7. **Accent colour.** A warm page takes a cool accent, and a cool page a
   warm one. Use it on one hero object and the primary button.
8. **Design skills compared.** The best result had no AI tells. The worst
   invented reviews and statistics. Never invent content.

## The ten ideas, ranked

1. **Closure at the edge.** Every stop leaves a slice of the next
   stretch of city in view, so going sideways needs no arrow.
2. **The move.** Slow in and out, interruptible, responding in under
   about 300 ms, rubber banding at both ends.
3. **One line.** A single connected line joins the six stops, with the
   current one lit.
4. **One warm accent on a cool city.** The lantern colour goes only on
   the primary action.
5. **Glass only where it has a reason.** Use it over the painting, and
   solid ground elsewhere.
6. **Run the unslop list as an audit.** One primary hero action, matched
   radii, no glow shadows, no identical cards, balanced wrapping, and not
   every section fading in the same way.
7. **Depth.** Parallax in the painting, with follow-through. Nothing
   moves in still view.
8. **Peak-end.** Contact is the best-lit stop, with a clear success
   moment (the email copied).
9. **Figure-ground.** The city dims behind the menu and the résumé
   dialog, and menus grow from their key.
10. **One quiet texture.** A single texture ties the stops into one city
    plan.
